## Key Decisions & Learnings — 25 May 2026

- **51% after 87% green is expected and healthy.** This is normal post-training absorption — not a relapse. The body processes the training stimulus as recovery drops, then rebuilds above the previous baseline. Monitoring the next 2–3 days to confirm an upward trend is the right response.
- **The week ended where it started (49–51% yellow) with the 87% green peak on Sunday.** The trend across the week: two reds → yellow → yellow → yellow → red → green → yellow. The second half of the week (Wed–Mon) was considerably better managed than the first.

### Training
- **50-minute strength session is a good weekday session length.** Efficient, sufficient stimulus, and within the yellow recovery budget. This duration + frequency (5 of 8 days trained) is a sustainable weekly cadence.
- **Monday strength after Sunday cardio is a sensible split.** Different stimulus types on consecutive days reduces the cumulative load on any single system (CNS vs cardiovascular).

### Nutrition
- **Two consecutive low-calorie days (Sun 1,411 + Mon 1,550) need correction.** After a high-strain Saturday and cardio Sunday, the body requires calorie surplus (or at minimum maintenance) to repair and adapt. Sub-1,600 kcal on back-to-back training days suppresses recovery.
- **The overnight oats as a late-night snack is a useful protein rescue mechanism** but should complement, not replace, structured meals. The pattern of using it to catch up at the end of the day suggests earlier meals weren't planned around the protein target.
- **Light dinners (cottage cheese + toast) work only when earlier meals are large.** With a Grenade Bar breakfast and Black Bean Tofu lunch, the evening meal needed to be more substantial to close the calorie and protein gap.

### Week Pattern (18–25 May)
- **Training streak:** 7 of 8 days with activity (only Thursday was rest). Sustainable in the short term, but the three reds suggest the load-to-recovery balance needs refinement.
- **Supplement compliance:** 6/7 or 7/7 on 6 of 8 days, with two missed evenings (Mon–Tue) directly correlating with the back-to-back reds. The lesson is clear and well-evidenced now.
- **Protein target hit:** 4 of 8 days (18, 19, 21, 22). The 4 misses were all under by 10–25g — not catastrophic, but a consistent pattern of slight underperformance.
- **The best day of the week (Sunday 87%) followed the best supplement compliance streak (4 full days).** Causality is reasonable to infer.
