## Key Decisions & Learnings — 23 May 2026

### Recovery
- **Long sleep duration ≠ good recovery.** 9.3h of sleep with 95min deep sleep produced a 39% red. Duration is not the only variable — deep sleep quality (driven by HRV, stress, nutrition, evening light exposure) is the primary determinant of physical recovery.
- **RHR at 65bpm on a red day post-8h sleep warrants investigation.** When elevated RHR persists despite adequate sleep, consider: dietary quality the previous day, hydration, cumulative training load over the week, or subclinical immune response.
- **Three reds this week (Mon, Tue, Sat) in an 8-day window indicates accumulated fatigue.** The week had two consecutive red days early, recovered mid-week, then hit red again Saturday. A full deload or rest block may be warranted after this period.

### Training
- **Push Day on a 39% red with 187bpm max HR is the riskiest training decision of the week.** The CNS suppression that drives a red recovery is most stressed by heavy compound pressing. The 17.0 strain generated confirms significant physiological cost.
- **The highest-strain day correlated with the lowest-recovery day.** This isn't coincidence — the body was already under stress, and high-intensity training deepened it.

### Nutrition
- **Missing breakfast on a training day is a meaningful deficit.** No breakfast + Push Day = under-fuelled session and 25g protein shortfall. Breakfast is the easiest, most controllable protein meal — skipping it removes the anchor.
- **Burro Blanco 1/2lb (48g protein) is the best restaurant protein source identified this period.** When eating out on training days, this should be the go-to dinner option.
